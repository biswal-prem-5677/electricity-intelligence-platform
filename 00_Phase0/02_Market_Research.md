# Market Research

## Market thesis

India’s smart-meter programme creates awareness of digital electricity data, while consumer and small-commercial users still need independent, local, circuit-level insight. The near-term opportunity is not to compete for utility billing meters; it is to serve customers who want a faster feedback loop and a serviceable retrofit.

The Ministry of Power reported 20.33 crore smart meters sanctioned under RDSS, with 4.93 crore installed as of December 2025. This supports a large ecosystem but also raises a boundary: consumer products must coexist with DISCOM equipment and cannot assume access to its data. ## Segments

| Segment | Willingness to pay | Sales motion | Product fit | Constraint |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Premium urban homes | Medium | D2C + electrician | Home-8, solar-ready | Panel variability and install friction |
| Small retail/restaurants | Medium-high | Installer/channel | Home-16 later | Need clear ROI and after-hours alert |
| Hostels/schools | Medium | B2B pilot | 3P/site later | Procurement and multi-tenant reporting |
| Hospitals/industry | High | Direct enterprise | Separate programme | Must not enter without certified 3P and support SLA |
| Government/DISCOM | Potentially large | Tender/partner | Not MVP | Long sales cycle and compulsory standards |

## Adoption barriers and answers

| Barrier | Design/business response |
|---|---|
| “My bill already shows units” | Demonstrate circuit-level evidence and a 7-day review, not generic savings claims |
| Unsafe panel work | Mandatory qualified installation; photo checklist and site rejection criteria |
| Uncertain tariff and savings | Tariff estimator labels itself as estimate and shows input assumptions |
| Wi-Fi may be weak | Pre-install signal test, local buffering, approved extender option |
| Privacy/occupancy concerns | Data minimisation, retention choices, no sale of personal telemetry |
| Upfront price sensitivity | Tiered 8/16 channel hardware, installer financing only after unit economics prove out |

## Market size approach

Do not publish a top-down market-size number until the launch geography, eligible-panel percentage, attach rate and price are evidenced. Use a bottom-up serviceable-market model:

TAM = eligible electrified premises × addressable share × annual hardware-and-service value.  
SAM = TAM × launch cities × qualified-installation availability.  
SOM = SAM × channel conversion × support capacity.

This avoids presenting analyst-market forecasts with incompatible definitions as a decision-grade number. The first commercial target is 1,000 paid monitored sites, not a percentage of a speculative global market.

## Technology and behaviour trends

Circuit monitors offer deterministic attribution; whole-home NILM remains attractive but must be treated as a confidence-ranked supplement. Consumers expect mobile access and no surprise subscription. Small businesses buy when the alert maps to an operational action: “cold-room circuit drew 1.2 kW for 3 hours after close,” not “AI detected inefficiency.”
